# Radiopharmaceuticals

## Definition
Cancer therapies that deliver a radioactive payload to tumor cells via a targeting molecule —
"a swarm of micro-drones" that bind a tumor marker, then detonate a ~100-micron (single-cell)
radiation blast with minimal collateral damage. **Alpha emitters** like **actinium-225** are
especially potent.

## How it works
- A targeting moiety (antibody, peptide, or **miniprotein**) recognizes a tumor antigen (e.g.
  nectin-4, B7-H3); a chelated radioisotope is the warhead.
- Target engagement is **imaging-confirmable** in early trials → de-risks development.

## Pros / cons
- **Pro:** Precision + a real **supply-chain moat** — actinium-225 is scarce (legacy U-233 /
  thorium-229 material), hard for generic/China competitors to replicate; strong pharma M&A
  appetite (Novartis, Bayer, BMS, Lilly).
- **Con:** Manufacturing/isotope supply constraints; binary clinical risk; biotech valuations
  are hard (risk-adjusted DCFs trend to zero).
