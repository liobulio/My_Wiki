# Activist investing

## Definition
Taking a meaningful stake in a company and then **pushing for change** — board seats, spin-offs,
cost cuts, capital return, strategy, or M&A — to close a gap between price and intrinsic/
realizable value.

## How it works
- **13G vs 13D:** crossing 5% ownership, you file a **13G** (passive) or a **13D** (intent to
  *engage*). The 13D is the activist's flag (engagement, not necessarily hostility).
- Lever change via the boardroom (large voting + economic stake → outsized voice), public
  campaigns (increasingly **on Twitter/X**), proxy fights, or takeover bids.
- Thesis usually rests on **value realization**: the assets/earnings power exist but management/
  governance is blocking it.

## Pros / cons
- **Pro:** a built-in **catalyst** (you can force the re-rate rather than wait); aligns price with
  value.
- **Con:** entrenchment (boards reject, raise thresholds, lawyer up); financing/credibility
  scrutiny; key-person dependent; can turn hostile and slow.
