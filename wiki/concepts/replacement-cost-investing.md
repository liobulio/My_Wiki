# Replacement-cost investing (buy a hard asset below replacement cost)

## Definition
Buying a productive **hard asset for less than it would cost to rebuild today**. The gap
between the purchase price and **replacement cost** (what it would take — land, materials,
labor, permitting — to recreate the asset's capacity now) is the **margin of safety**. You
profit as the price converges toward, and eventually exceeds, replacement cost — especially
when the world needs *more* of that capacity but building new is slow and expensive.

## How it works
- **Replacement cost** = the all-in cost to build equivalent capacity today (not book value,
  not historical cost). Related to **Tobin's Q** (market value ÷ replacement cost; `Q < 1` = the
  market values the asset below the cost to rebuild it).
- Buy when **EV(enterprise value) < replacement cost**. With debt in the capital structure, the **equity** gets
  leveraged upside to closing that gap.
- The thesis pays off when (a) demand requires **new** capacity, and (b) building new is
  **more expensive / slower** than buying existing assets → existing assets **re-rate up**
  toward (or past) replacement cost.
- **Sam Zell's playbook** (the canonical articulation): buy a hard asset at a discount to
  replacement cost, **hold**, and sell it at a **premium** to replacement cost when the market
  wakes up.

## Pros / cons
- **Pro:** tangible **floor** / margin of safety — downside is anchored to real asset value, not
  sentiment. **Asymmetric** when a build-out cycle arrives (you own cheap capacity nobody can
  cheaply reproduce).
- **Con — value trap:** replacement cost is irrelevant if **nobody needs the capacity** — the
  gap can persist indefinitely without a demand catalyst.
- **Con — obsolescence:** if the asset/technology is being **replaced** (not rebuilt), its
  replacement cost is moot.
- **Con — estimation & rates:** replacement cost is an estimate; long-dated real assets are
  **interest-rate sensitive** (discounting) and exposed to **regulatory** caps.

## Who uses it
- **Sam Zell** — bought real estate below replacement cost; **Equity Office Properties** sold to
  Blackstone near the 2007 peak (~$39B) — buy-low-sell-at-a-premium executed.
- **[[dan-dreyfus]]** ([[tln|Talen Energy]] pitch): ~$25B EV vs ~$45B replacement cost into a
  structural [[ai-power-demand|power shortage]] — the textbook setup (cheap hard asset + a
  demand catalyst that makes new builds expensive).
- Real-asset / value investors generally (power, infrastructure, real estate, mines, ships).
