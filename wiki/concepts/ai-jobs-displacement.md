# AI jobs: displacement vs loss

## Definition
The debate over AI's labor impact, and the distinction the besties converge on: **job
*displacement* (roles shift/retire) ≠ aggregate job *loss***. Present-day data shows no net loss.

## The evidence
- **RAMP / Revelio study** (21,559 US firms): heavy AI adopters grew headcount **+10.2%** over 2
  yrs (entry-level **+12%**); gains span sales/admin/finance/customer service. Non-adopters were
  flat. Correlation, not causation — but no job-loss signal.
- **Klarna reversal:** walked back replacing customer service with AI (handled volume, not
  complexity) → rehiring humans. Feeds the "**human-in-the-loop premium**" idea (human interaction
  gets *more* valuable / commands a premium as routine work automates).
- **Displacement is coming** (Calacanis): customer support, data entry/BPO, drivers
  (Waymo/Optimus) — but gradually, and offset by new roles / a startup "Cambrian explosion."

## Why it matters (investment relevance)
- Tempers the "AI = mass unemployment" narrative used to justify heavy AI regulation
  ([[ai-gatekeeping]] tie-in) — [[david-friedberg]]'s framing.
- **Long "human-in-the-loop"** premium services; watch robotics (Optimus/Figure) for the genuine
  displacement in warehouses/last-mile.
