# California fiscal risk

## Definition
The thesis ([[david-friedberg]]) that California's "balanced budget" is an accounting illusion
masking a **structural deficit + tax-base flight + massive unfunded liabilities** — a slow-moving
muni-credit / tax-migration risk.

## The argument
- **"Balanced" via borrowing:** Newsom signed a ~**$351B** budget (claimed $0 deficit through
  2028), but LAO/CalMatters flag **~$20–35B/yr** out-year structural deficits papered over with debt.
- **Tax-base concentration & exodus:** top rate **14.4%** (highest in US); the **top 1%** (~150k
  people) pay ~half of personal income tax. 15 Fortune 500 HQs + ~2,100 mid/large firms left since
  2019; AGI leaving ~1–1.5%/yr. New taxes (8% software sales tax, health-insurance tax; a proposed
  **billionaire tax**) push more out.
- **Liabilities:** Friedberg cites ~$1.4T public debt + ~$664B (maybe ~$1.5T) unfunded pensions +
  ~$175B retiree healthcare; pensions sit **senior to bonds** (the "California rule").
- **Tail risk:** default → federal bailout → red states question the union (Friedberg's macro
  scenario); Chamath expects a negotiated pension wipeout/restructuring instead.

## Why it matters (investment relevance)
- **CA muni credit** risk; **tax migration** to **TX/FL** (0% / lower rates) as a durable trend;
  a **billionaire/wealth-tax** overhang on high earners and founders.
