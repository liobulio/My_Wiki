# Quant Corner Ep. 47 — Does Momentum Still Work?

> ⚠️ **Illustrative sample source** seeded for the first-ingest demo. Content reflects real,
> well-established research; the show/host are fictional. Safe to delete.

## TL;DR
A primer on the [[momentum-factor]]: assets that outperformed over the trailing 6–12 months
tend to keep outperforming short-term. Real and persistent across asset classes, but it
carries crash risk and works as a **diversifier**, not a standalone. [[mtum]] is the standard
retail vehicle.

## Key takeaways
- `[00:10]` Momentum = trailing 6–12m winners keep winning short-term; documented across
  stocks, bonds, FX, commodities.
- `[03:40]` Canonical reference: Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen, *Value and Momentum Everywhere*
  (Journal of Finance, 2013). [[cliff-asness]] / [[AQR]] central to the research.
- `[03:40]` Value and momentum are **negatively correlated** → combining them smooths returns.
- `[11:25]` **Momentum crashes** (Daniel & Moskowitz): fat left tail, worst in sharp post-crash
  rebounds (spring 2009). You're paid for taking crash risk.
- `[18:05]` [[mtum]] (iShares MSCI USA Momentum Factor ETF) = simplest US implementation; low
  expense ratio; semi-annual reconstitution + volatility-triggered rebalances.
- `[24:30]` Verdict: real premium, but a diversifier — pair with [[value-factor]], size for
  crash risk, expect long flat stretches.

## Notable claims
- Value/momentum negative correlation is the basis for combining them (AQR research).
- Momentum's worst drawdowns cluster in violent market recoveries (short side blows up).
- ETF-wrapped momentum is lower-turnover / "watered-down" vs academic momentum.
