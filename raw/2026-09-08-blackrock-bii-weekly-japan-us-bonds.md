# Why Japan matters for U.S. bond investors (BlackRock Investment Institute weekly commentary)
# 机构: BlackRock Investment Institute · 发布: 2026-09-08 · URL: https://www.blackrock.com/us/individual/insights/blackrock-investment-institute/weekly-commentary
# 说明: WebFetch 结构化摘要 + 两句原文引用；非全文。BII 主题页 https://www.blackrock.com/corporate/insights/blackrock-investment-institute 同日抓取（Wei Li 三大主题）。

Main argument: Japan's government bond yields (~3%, highest in 30 years) create attractive domestic returns that could draw Japanese capital home from U.S. Treasuries. Japan holds ~$1.1 trillion of U.S. Treasuries; a hypothetical 5% repatriation = $55 billion ≈ 25% of annual foreign Treasury inflows and 7% of quarterly Treasury net borrowing.

Tactical views (6–12 months): U.S. equities overweight (+1); long U.S. Treasuries underweight (−1); Japanese government bonds underweight (−1); U.S. agency MBS overweight (+1); EM local currency overweight (+1); Japanese equities neutral; global high yield neutral.

Quotes: "Japan still holds roughly $1.1 trillion of U.S. Treasuries." / "Higher rates are not uniformly bearish for risk assets: we favor companies with the earnings and cash flows to outrun a higher cost of capital."

BII landing page — three themes (Wei Li, Global Chief Investment Strategist): (1) AI infrastructure — "opportunities where bottlenecks are appearing: power, grids, chips and data centers," robotics the next frontier; (2) Durable income — higher yields make income attractive; short-duration, credit backed by resilient cash flows; (3) Beyond traditional asset classes — start from themes and risks, then choose the access route. Also listed: "Energy security in a world shaped by supply" (2026-06-22, Kaminker et al.) — electricity systems are local/real-time; AI, data centers, EVs drive structural power demand; bottlenecks in generation, transmission, storage, grid; nuclear has the longest deployment timeline.
